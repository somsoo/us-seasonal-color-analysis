📖 Color Theory: The 12-Season Munsell Framework

Seasonal color analysis applies Albert Munsell's scientific color coordinate system (Hue, Value, and Chroma) to human undertones and facial pigmentation:

  • Temperature (Hue): Cool skin possesses blue/pink hemoglobin overtones; warm skin reflects golden/yellow melanin overtones. Wearing matching temperature garments neutralizes sallow shadows and dark under-eye circles.
  • Value (Lightness vs Darkness): Measures the overall depth and contrast between hair, iris, and skin. High-contrast features thrive in vivid, saturated jewel tones; lower-contrast features harmonize in delicate pastels or muted earth tones.
  • Chroma (Clarity vs Softness): Determines whether your features are complemented by pure, saturated pigment (Clear/Bright) or complex greyed/browned nuances (Muted/Soft).

Color theory references: Munsell A. (1905) A Color Notation; Jackson C. (1980) Color Me Beautiful; Spillane M. (1991) Color Me Confident.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How does the 12-season color analysis system work?

The system evaluates three dimensions of Munsell color theory: Temperature (Warm vs Cool), Value (Light vs Dark), and Chroma (Bright/Clear vs Muted/Soft) to classify you into Spring, Summer, Autumn, or Winter.

How do I determine if my skin undertone is warm or cool?

Cool undertones have blue/purple wrist veins, look best in silver jewelry, and burn easily in sun. Warm undertones have greenish veins, look best in yellow gold, and tan easily.

What is the difference between Summer and Winter palettes?

Both seasons share cool undertones, but Summer features soft, muted, and lighter pastel shades, whereas Winter demands high contrast, deep jewel tones, and vivid pure bright colors.
